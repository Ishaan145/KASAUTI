# Build KASAUTI, but shrink the claim

**Verdict: feasible, and better-positioned than most hackathon entries — but only if you strip two claims out of the pitch before you write a line of code.** The data layer clears: SEBI publishes complete, dated registers of 1,062 Investment Advisers and 2,324 Research Analysts as on 25 Oct 2026, and NSE publishes the consolidated SEBI-debarred-entities list as two direct-download .XLS files, so both the positive signal (is this person registered?) and the negative signal (is this person barred?) are obtainable inside a day of plumbing work ([SEBI IA list](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebiweb/other/OtherAction.do?doRecognisedFpi=yes&intmId=13), [NSE debarred entities](https://www.nseindia.com/regulations/member-sebi-debarred-entities)). What does **not** clear is Gate 2: Meta publishes **no per-language WER or accuracy figure for any Indian language on any Muse model** — Hindi is a listed, "extensively verified" ASR language with zero published number attached to it, and the only independent Hinglish datapoint found shows Muse Voice Transcribe at **54.21% WER versus Whisper's 30.84%** on a single mixed-script clip ([Meta speech-to-text docs](https://dev.meta.ai/docs/speech-to-text), [Kingy.ai](https://kingy.ai/blog/meta-muse-voice-transcribe-guide/)). That is one clip, not a ranking, but it means your Hinglish performance is an **empirical question you must answer on day 1, not an assumption you can build on**. The positioning is genuinely defensible in exactly three places — act-type-and-status classification rather than truth classification, code-mixed as the primary input rather than an afterthought, and calibrated abstention with a reported coverage number — and indefensible everywhere else, because SEBI itself shipped credential verification on 1 October 2025 and "AI misinformation detector" is the single most cloned hackathon pitch in existence. The two claims to delete: that KASAUTI verifies whether a tip is *true*, and that finfluencer content *caused* SEBI's documented ₹1.05 lakh crore of F&O losses. Neither survives contact with the evidence.

## Gate 1 clears, but SEBI gives you HTML and a permission clause, never an API

**There is no SEBI API, and nothing suggests one is coming.** The registration data lives behind a single legacy Java servlet, `OtherAction.do`, with Investment Advisers at `intmId=13` (**1,062 records**) and Research Analysts at `intmId=14` (**2,324 records**), both stamped "Registered intermediaries as on date Oct 25, 2026" and both fetched live on 26 Oct 2026 ([SEBI IA list](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebiweb/other/OtherAction.do?doRecognisedFpi=yes&intmId=13), [SEBI RA list](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebiweb/other/OtherAction.do?doRecognisedFpi=yes&intmId=14)). Page 1 of each category is a clean GET with no session needed. Everything past that is not: pagination links call a JavaScript function (`searchFormFpi('n','1')` for page 2, zero-indexed) rather than carrying hrefs, the Download column on the Recognised Intermediaries index is present but **every cell in it is empty**, and appending a search value as a GET parameter is silently ignored — a test fetch of `&intmId=13&searchValue=INA000017523` returned blank search boxes and the full unfiltered count of 1,062 ([SEBI Recognised Intermediaries](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebiweb/other/OtherAction.do?doRecognised=yes)). The architectural consequence is forced and, as it happens, correct: **mirror the whole registry nightly and query locally**. At 3,386 combined records that is a small, fast local index, and it removes any per-user dependency on SEBI uptime.

Your single highest-value first test is the undocumented **"Show All Records"** control (`searchAllIntm()`) on the list pages. If it dumps all rows in one response, the entire registry is two requests instead of 136 paginated ones. That is unverified — flag it as the researcher did — and so are the POST field names behind `searchFormFpi()`, because the rendered-text fetch tool used for this research strips `<form>` tags and direct curl to `www.sebi.gov.in` was blocked by the research environment's egress proxy. **Those are gaps, not negatives**: a browser dev-tools session resolves all of them in under an hour, and you should do it before writing any crawler.

The enforcement side is easier and is the quickest real win in the whole build. NSE publishes the consolidated SEBI-debarred list at `https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/content/press/prs_ra_sebi.xls` (~1,042 KB) and entities debarred by other authorities at `prs_ra_others.xls` (~319 KB) — two stable URLs, binary Excel, no pagination, no session ([NSE debarred entities](https://www.nseindia.com/regulations/member-sebi-debarred-entities)). That the data is tractable is proven by **OpenSanctions, which ingests exactly those files daily into clean CSV/JSON — 30,783 entities, 14,434 targets, last processed 2026-10-26** ([OpenSanctions in_nse_debarred](https://opensanctions.org/datasets/in_nse_debarred/)). Use OpenSanctions as a dev-time sanity check and a name-normalisation exemplar, **not as your data source**: it is CC BY-NC 4.0, so a commercial product needs a licence deal with no published price ([OpenSanctions licensing](https://www.opensanctions.org/licensing/)). Build against NSE directly. Two caveats carried forward from the research: the `.xls` files are legacy BIFF and need `xlrd<2.0` or conversion, and the NSE page carries a single ambiguous "Updated on: 07/08/2025" stamp with no stated schedule — poll and diff rather than trusting freshness. **The column schema of those two files was never parsed** (the research environment could not download them), so whether they contain PAN, registration numbers, or debarment start/end dates is unknown. That is your other first-hour test.

Four things are confirmed **not** available and should not appear in your architecture. **SEBI Check is not a registration lookup** — it verifies UPI handles and bank account + IFSC for payment channels, and name and registration number are not inputs ([SEBI Check](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_check.html)); note the live SI Portal page showed no form fields at all on a text-only fetch, so its internals are unknown. The **Specified Digital Platform** framework has been at consultation stage since Oct 2024 with a comment deadline of 26 Nov 2024 and still has no notified list — treat it as vapour ([MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2024/10/223-sebi-consultation-paper-on-recognition-as-specified-digital-platform/)). **BASL publishes no member list**: the BSE page at `basl_member.aspx` is an enlistment policy with no names and no search form, and `bseasl.com` is robots-disallowed ([BSE BASL page](https://bseindia.com/static/iara/basl_member.aspx)). And **AMFI's ARN data is both inaccessible and off-axis** — a finfluencer giving buy/sell calls needs an INA or INH registration; ARN only authorises mutual fund distribution ([AMFI Distributor Corner](https://www.amfiindia.com/distributor-corner)). Defer AMFI entirely.

Two legal items go on day 1, not day 9. SEBI's Website Policy states that material may be reproduced free of charge **after taking permission by email**, with the source prominently acknowledged ([SEBI Website Policy](https://www.sebi.gov.in/website-policy.html)). The policy says nothing about scraping, bots or text-and-data mining, which means the live constraint is reproduction and copyright, not contractual ToS — and absence of an anti-scraping clause is not permission. Checking a name against cached SEBI data at query time sits comfortably; republishing the 3,386 records as your own browsable directory does not. Send the permission email on day 1, display "Source: SEBI, as on <date>" on every verdict, and link back to the SEBI list page. Separately, **`sebi.gov.in/robots.txt` could not be retrieved in this research** — read it yourself before your crawler runs, and crawl conservatively (serialised, 1–2s delay, nightly refresh, never per-user hits) against what is a legacy JSP stack with no published rate limits.

The sharpest finding in Gate 1 is a silence. A web search for SEBI intermediary scrapers returned **no SEBI repositories at all** — every result was US SEC IAPD or BrokerCheck tooling — and no Stack Overflow questions or Indian developer posts about `OtherAction.do` surfaced either. Treat that as "none discoverable via web search," moderate confidence, not proof of absence; the researcher could not run a direct GitHub code search and recommends you do. But the steelman cuts both ways: **the absence may reflect low demand and small data as much as difficulty** — 3,386 records is a list a hobbyist can eyeball. Which is exactly why the registry mirror is not your moat.

## Gate 2 does not clear: Meta documents Hindi support and publishes zero Hindi numbers

Be direct with yourself here, because this is where over-claiming would be easiest. **Meta publishes no per-language accuracy or WER figure for any Indian language on any Muse model. The multilingual support claims are documented support *lists*, not documented *performance*.**

Muse Voice Transcribe's docs do list six Indian languages — **Hindi, Bengali, Marathi, Tamil, Telugu, Kannada** — among 25 "extensively verified" of 70+ trained, and both the docs and the dev blog claim code-switching support ([Meta speech-to-text](https://dev.meta.ai/docs/speech-to-text), [research.meta.ai](https://research.meta.ai/blog/introducing-muse-voice-transcribe)). But the published WER is **a single English-only aggregate of 3.1%** on an unnamed dataset, with a separate chart reporting ~3.0% at 0.16s time-to-final — a discrepancy Meta does not explain. Code-switching is described qualitatively with one Mandarin–English demo and **no numeric result for any language pair**. Diarization has a published aggregate DER of 17.5% across AMI-IHM, AMI-SDM and VoxConverse with no per-dataset breakdown. Accented English is not evaluated at all.

The one Indian-language datapoint anywhere is independent, single-clip, and self-labelled as not generalisable: on a 5-minute Hindi–English mixed-script MUCS clip, **Muse scored 54.21% "WER-style" against Whisper's 30.84%**, and setting `languageBias` to Hindi+English moved it to 54.39% — **no improvement**. On a 25-minute English aggregate the ordering flipped (Muse 11.93%, Whisper 15.34%) ([Kingy.ai](https://kingy.ai/blog/meta-muse-voice-transcribe-guide/)). Kingy explicitly cautions this is "one script-sensitive clip, not a general Hindi ranking" and attributes most of the gap to Muse transliterating English terms into Devanagari, which token matching scores as error. That mechanism matters more than the number: **if the dominant Hinglish error source is script choice rather than acoustic accuracy, raw WER is the wrong evaluation frame**, and you should score with transliteration-aware or script-normalised matching. The `languageBias` parameter is documented as a hint that "does not force" language, and the one test available suggests it does not control output script.

For text, the finding is starker. **Muse Spark has no published supported-language list at all** — not on the model page, not on either launch blog, and the Spark 1.1 Evaluation Report contains no Global-MMLU, MMMLU, Flores, Belebele, IndicGLUE or IndicMMLU results ([Muse Spark](https://dev.meta.ai/models/muse-spark), [Muse Spark 1.1 Evaluation Report](https://ai.meta.com/static-resource/muse-spark-1-1-evaluation-report/)). The Muse Spark Open model card says the model was trained on data from 100+ languages, gives no list, and states plainly that it "has not been evaluated on all languages contained in the pre-training data" ([HF Muse-Spark-Open](https://huggingface.co/meta-models/Muse-Spark-Open-816B-A42B)). The **only** place Romanized Hindi appears in Meta's primary material is an interpreter-abuse refusal table — Muse Spark 1.1 scoring 0.60 on Hindi and 1.16 on Hindi (Romanized) attack success rate. **That is a safety measurement, not a capability measurement.** Any claim that Meta "benchmarked Hindi" rests on a category error, and you should say so explicitly if a judge raises it. One unresolved item: the evaluation report was truncated before its General Capabilities section, so multilingual capability benchmarks could exist there unretrieved — flagged as unresolved rather than absent.

So what does the adjacent literature **predict**? Three convergent signals, all about models that are not Muse — this is inference, clearly labelled. First, **Indi-RomCoM** evaluates Romanized code-mixed Indic-English instructions across Hindi, Bengali, Gujarati and Tamil at 25/50/75% code-mixing intensity, and finds LLaMA-3.1-70B dropping from a 54.6% English baseline to 50.5% at 75% mixing, with LLaMA-3.2-1B collapsing from 24.3% to 11.6% ([arXiv: Indi-RomCoM](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.30790)). Do not read the Llama models' small 2.2–4.0 point drops as robustness: those are **floor effects from already-low baselines**, and the ±32–36 standard deviations show wildly uneven per-task performance. The paper covers the LLaMa3 series — **not Llama 4, not Muse**. Second, GLUECoS puts mBERT at **57.51 on EN-HI sentiment and 57.74 on NLI** — barely above chance for those task shapes ([GLUECoS](https://microsoft.github.io/GLUECoS/)); that is July 2020 and almost certainly not 2026 SOTA, so do not present it as current, but it anchors the difficulty. Third, and most directly relevant to an LLM-centric pipeline: on code-mixed NER, fine-tuned **HingBERT reaches 79.74 F1 while zero-shot Gemini reaches 62.24** — a ~15-point gap favouring a small code-mixed-specific encoder over prompting a frontier model ([arXiv 2509.02514](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.02514v1)). Note also the ordering HingBERT > MuRIL (77.50) > IndicBERT (76.47): code-mixed-specific pretraining beats Indic-general pretraining on romanised Hinglish.

**The honest Gate 2 position is "unknown, with a predicted ceiling well below English."** Muse is a different architecture (MoE plus Perception Encoder) with an undisclosed data mix, so extrapolating any of the above to it is unsupported. The actionable consequence is that **you must run a 30-example Hinglish smoke test against Muse Voice Transcribe and Muse Spark 1.2 before you commit the architecture** — and that if ASR quality on voice notes is bad, you need a fallback path decided in advance rather than discovered on day 8. Expect high-50s-to-low-80s territory depending on task shape, and **pre-empt the obvious criticism: if you report 90%+ on a Hinglish semantic classification task, assume a judge will conclude your test set is leaky.**

## The white space is act-type and abstention, not truth — and the regulator already owns credential lookup

Two of the three things KASAUTI might claim are already taken.

**Credential verification is occupied by the regulator itself.** SEBI announced SEBI Check in an 11 June 2025 circular and it went live **1 October 2025**, distributed through SEBI's own Saa₹thi app, alongside NPCI-issued `@valid` UPI handles with `.brk` and `.mf` suffixes granted exclusively to SEBI-registered intermediaries ([Groww](https://groww.in/blog/what-are-validated-upi-handles-and-sebi-check-tool)). A pitch whose core novelty is "look up whether this adviser is registered" reads to a judge as reinventing SEBI Check. **Truth verification is crowded academic ground.** The FinNLP-FNP-LLMFinLegal 2025 Financial Misinformation Detection shared task ran on 1,952 train / 1,304 test claims labelled True/False/Not-Enough-Information, with 32 teams registered and the winner (Dunamu ML, using GPT-4 evidence generation, FAISS few-shot selection and a fine-tuned Llama-3.1-8B) reaching **Micro-F1 0.8467** ([FinNLP 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.finnlp-1.30.pdf)). There are purpose-built models — FMDLlama, and MFMDQwen at 0.818 average F1 across a 9-task benchmark ([arXiv 2604.18272](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.18272)). And "AI misinformation detector" as a hackathon genre is maximally cloned: three unrelated projects named TruthLens, and India's own TruthTell hackathon drew **5,600 registrations** on exactly this theme ([HackerEarth TruthTell](https://hackerearth.com/challenges/hackathon/truth-tell-hackathon/custom-tab/evaluation-parameter)).

The defensible seam is visible in the regulators' own account of what they currently do. **IOSCO's Final Report FR/08/2025 (May 2025) says almost nothing about AI or ML**: Belgium's FSMA built an in-house web scraper that flags social-media crypto ads without separating compliant from non-compliant, so staff manually filter; Spain's CNMV reviewed a *sample* of finfluencer activity in 2022; Canada's CSA plans electronic-media analysis with no named tool. IOSCO lists data analytics and social-media surveillance as **recommended good practice — an aspiration, not described current practice** ([IOSCO FR/08/2025](https://www.iosco.org/library/pubdocs/pdf/IOSCOPD795.pdf)). Meanwhile the published ML literature targets **truth**; the regulatory need is **act-type and status** — is this utterance a recommendation, and is the speaker registered? Those are different labels, and nothing found treats the second as an ML classification task with a benchmark. Reinforcing it: **MFMDQwen's multilingual coverage is English, Chinese, Bengali and Greek — explicitly no Hindi and no code-mixed** ([arXiv 2604.18272](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.18272)). That is the single cleanest citable hole for an India-focused project.

Against the judging weights, here is where I would spend the effort.

| Criterion | Weight | What actually scores | What loses |
|---|---|---|---|
| Impact | 30% | SEBI's own ₹1.05 lakh crore / 88% loss-rate data; 70,000 takedowns as a volume argument; 13 crore investors, median age 33 | Asserting finfluencers caused the losses — a judge who checks will find no source says that |
| Use of Meta AI tooling | 25% | Three models doing real work: Voice Transcribe on voice notes, Spark 1.2 for multimodal claim extraction, SAM 3.1 on YouTube Short frames to find or fail to find the disclosure overlay | A thin Spark wrapper; or using Muse Image, which you are prohibited from accessing from India |
| Technical depth & execution | 20% | Conformal abstention with reported **empirical coverage, set-size distribution, and language-stratified coverage**; the registry ∧ enforcement join; a Hinglish eval with transliteration-aware scoring | Claiming a coverage guarantee over LLM free generation — exchangeability breaks and a reviewer will catch it |
| Demo | 25% | Forward a real Hinglish voice note, watch it decompose into claim / recommendation / guarantee, see the disclosure checklist resolve, and see one case **abstain** | A happy-path-only reel; no abstention shown |

**Two framing moves for a panel unfamiliar with India.** First, lead with the fact that nine national securities regulators ran a coordinated finfluencer enforcement week in June 2025, producing 3 arrests, 6 criminal proceedings and 650 takedown requests ([FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/fca-cracks-down-illegal-promotions-and-market-abuse-first-year-new-strategy)) — that establishes the problem is a recognised global category before you make it specific to India. Second, frame the output as **triage, not adjudication**. Brokers have conspicuously not built content-analysis features, and the plausible reason is liability: classifying a named person's speech as unregistered advice is a legal exposure a broker does not want. That is a real reason the white space exists and a real reason your product must say "this post is missing the SEBI disclosures required by the Advertisement Code" rather than "this person is a fraud." It is also useful that the established benchmark already carries a Not-Enough-Information class — **"cannot determine" is a legitimate output in this field, not a dodge**, which makes conformal abstention continuous with the literature rather than exotic.

One honesty note on prior art. The Hugging Face Hinglish scam-text datasets (`hinglish-scam-text-dataset` under multiple user accounts, `Indian_Cyber_Scam_PhoneCall_Hinglish_Dataset` likewise duplicated) appear to be forks or copies of a small number of originals, plausibly LLM-generated ([HF Yash1072006](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Yash1072006/hinglish-scam-text-dataset)). **Citing them as prior art strengthens your credibility; training on them without provenance inspection would destroy it.** And on fact-checking capacity: BOOM and Factly are active but no automated financial-misinformation tooling from them was found — state that as "we found no shipped automated tool," not "none exists." The researcher did not verify whether either runs internal claim-matching automation; carry that forward as open.

**Now the boundaries, and these are not stylistic — they are the difference between a credible submission and one a checking judge dismantles.**

**No source causally links finfluencer content to SEBI's documented F&O trading losses.** The SEBI derivatives data establishes the magnitude of harm in the market that misinformation operates in. It does not establish that misinformation caused those losses, and SEBI does not claim it does. Every time the ₹1.05 lakh crore or the 88% loss rate appears in your deck, it must be framed as the size of the arena, with any bridge to finfluencer content labelled as your inference. This is the single biggest evidentiary gap in the whole pack.

Four further non-combinations. SEBI's F&O losses (₹1.05 lakh crore, FY25) and I4C's investment-fraud losses are **different harms** — legal-but-ruinous trading versus criminal fraud — and must not be summed. The I4C 77% share and the FCRF 36% Southeast-Asia-linked share use **different bases**; do not combine. SEBI's 70,000 takedowns and the FCA's 650 takedown requests are **not methodologically comparable** (a cumulative multi-month platform-cooperation count versus a single coordinated operation). And the August 2026 SEBI study's FY25/FY26 net-loss figures (₹12,718 lakh / ₹12,407 lakh) are **sample-level for a 5,050-trader sample, not national aggregates** — the latest population aggregate is FY25's ₹1,05,603 crore, and there is **no published population-level FY26 aggregate**. One derived figure, flagged as derived: applying the 77% share to ₹19,812 crore gives **~₹15,255 crore of 2025 investment-fraud losses — a calculation, not a published I4C number.**

The vernacular argument is your weakest evidentiary leg and you should know it. The backbone statistics — **234 million Indian-language internet users at end-2016 versus 175 million English, projected to 536 million (~75% of the base) by 2021, and 68% of Indian internet users rating local-language content more reliable than English** — come from a Google-KPMG study published **April 2017**, with the forward figures being 2021 projections ([KPMG-Google](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmg/in/pdf/2017/04/Indian-languages-Defining-Indias-Internet.pdf)). That is roughly nine years old and no recent primary replacement was found. **Date it explicitly rather than presenting it as current.** Worse for positioning: **no study was found quantifying financial misinformation volume by language, and nothing at all was found on voice notes as a vector for financial misinformation** — this was searched for specifically. If KASAUTI's pitch rests on voice notes, it rests on a reasoned hypothesis, and you should say "hypothesis." Also unverified: the widely-headlined SEBI survey finding that Indian investors prefer social-media influencers to traditional advisers, because the two Moneylife articles reporting it returned client errors. **Obtain the primary SEBI Investor Survey 2025 report** — it would convert your single weakest link into your strongest.

The regulatory hook that *is* solid, and that connects regulation to the vernacular dimension, is the Advertisement Code. **Circular SEBI/HO/MIRSD/MIRSD-PoD-2/P/CIR/2023/51, issued 5 April 2023, effective 1 May 2023** requires a registered IA or RA to display their SEBI-registered name, registered office address, **SEBI registration number**, logo/brand name, CIN where applicable, and the standard risk warning in **at least 10-point font** with no words added or removed; for audio-visual ads the warning must appear on screen **and** be voiced over audibly; for **non-English ads it must be accurately translated**; for SMS, pop-ups or social posts that cannot carry full details, a hyperlink to a compliant official website is mandatory; example securities must be labelled "for illustration only"; and prior approval from a recognised supervisory body is required before issuing an ad ([SEBI Advertisement Code](https://aria.org.in/beta/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/SEBI-Advertisement-code-for-Investment-Advisers-IA-and-Research-Analysts-RA1.pdf)). Prohibited content includes promises of assured, minimum or target returns, reference to the adviser's own past performance, superlatives like "Best" or "No. 1", and use of the SEBI logo. Separately, finfluencer-related regulations took effect **29 August 2024**, and a **29 January 2025** circular restricted "educators" to price data with a **three-month lag** and barred registered entities from associating with finfluencers for any compensation ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/sebi-finfluencer-circular-live-stock-data-market-education-rules-125013000571_1.html)). **That three-month lag rule is the most machine-checkable provision in Indian finfluencer regulation** — a post pairing a stock name with live or recent prices is prima facie non-compliant unless the poster is registered. Two open items: the exact circular number and gazette date of the 29 Jan 2025 circular were not captured, and the Advertisement Code was not re-confirmed against sebi.gov.in for amendments between 2023 and Oct 2026.

## What could sink the build: identity resolution, a lapsed-registration false positive, and one guarantee you cannot make

**The real engineering risk is not data access — it is identity resolution.** SEBI's registry contains no social handles. Enforcement data is **name-keyed, not registration-number-keyed**, so joining debarments to the registry is a fuzzy-matching problem rather than a key lookup. And mapping "this Instagram handle" or "this Telegram channel" to a legal entity in the SEBI list is addressed by **no source found anywhere in this research**. That is where your ten days will actually go, and you should plan for it explicitly rather than discovering it on day 6.

**The correctness trap that would be worse than shipping nothing: reporting "registered" for a lapsed adviser.** The SEBI list has a Validity field, but whether it carries "Perpetual" or an expiry date, and whether suspended-but-not-cancelled registrations still appear on the main list, is **unknown — it requires parsing a real dump**. Note too that SEBI cancelled the registration of 68 investment advisers for non-payment of renewal fees in Nov 2025 ([NewsOnAir](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/sebi-cancels-registration-of-68-investment-advisers-over-non-payment-of-renewal-fees/)), so lapses are routine, not hypothetical. A separate "Cancelled/Surrendered/Expired/Suspended Intermediaries" page exists at `OtherAction.do?doRecognisedFpiFilter2=yes` and should be ingested alongside the main lists. Regex validation is a related trap and is **near-worthless as a trust signal on its own** — a fraudster mints a format-valid `INA000000001` trivially. Observed live values include `INA000017523`, `INA200000365`, `INA100005050` and the IFSC variant **`INAIFSC10001`**, plus `INH300000211` and `INH000011431`, so a digits-only regex produces false negatives and any pattern pinning the third character will too (it appears to encode a regional office — inference from four observed values, undocumented by SEBI). Use a permissive validator like `^IN[AH][A-Z0-9]{8,9}$` and then confirm against the actual registry. Whether `INHIFSC`-style RA variants exist is unobserved and not ruled out.

**Hard Meta-platform constraints.** Muse Spark 1.3's docs state plainly that "audio understanding in Muse Spark 1.3 is not fully supported" and recommend **1.2 or Muse Voice Transcribe** for audio ([Meta models](https://dev.meta.ai/docs/models)) — a developer who defaults to "newest version" gets degraded audio, which for KASAUTI is the core input. **Muse Image is explicitly prohibited from being accessed from India or made available to end users there**, the only India-specific restriction in the lineup ([Geographic Use Policy](https://dev.meta.ai/legal/geographic-use-policy)); Spark, Wonder, Voice Transcribe and SAM carry no India restriction. More subtly, **Meta publishes no positive country allow-list at all**, so India's general API availability is officially unconfirmed and reserves the right to change — **provision a key and make a live call before committing the architecture.** ASR has real shape constraints: raw mono 16-bit PCM at 16 or 24 kHz only (WAV/MP3/Opus must be decoded client-side), **32 MB max body, 10 minutes max per file, 60 minutes max realtime session with no resume token**, and **turn-level timestamps only — no word-level timestamps, no confidence scores** ([Meta speech-to-text](https://dev.meta.ai/docs/speech-to-text)). That means an ffmpeg stage plus a chunking-and-stitching layer you can only align on turn boundaries. SAM 3.1 takes **one short noun phrase per call** with no documented point/box/mask refinement, so a wrong mask can only be addressed by rephrasing, and there are no published benchmarks, resolution limits or failure modes on its page ([SAM 3.1](https://dev.meta.ai/models/sam-3-1)).

Cost is not your binding constraint; engineering time is. Documented unit prices: Voice Transcribe **$0.18/hour**, SAM video **$0.20 per 1,000 frames** (minimum 50 frames billed), SAM images **$2.50 per 1,000** — 12.5x the per-frame price, so route still batches through the video endpoint where possible — and Muse Spark Standard **$1.25/M input, $4.25/M output**, with the Contributor tier at $0.10/$0.20 but **150 RPM instead of 3,000** and with content potentially used for training ([Pricing and rate limits](https://dev.meta.ai/docs/pricing-rate-limits)). A per-item pipeline of ten minutes of ASR plus a 60-frame SAM pass plus one ~4k-in/1k-out Spark call works out to roughly **$0.05 per item, about 2,900 items for $150** — arithmetic from the documented prices, not a Meta figure. Two caveats that matter more than the arithmetic: **Meta documents no $150 credit grant, or any free credits at all** — if you are working to that number, it is an external premise, not a documented Meta offer. And **how images and video convert into billable tokens is entirely undocumented**, which is the largest hole in any multimodal cost model, because video-into-context could plausibly dwarf every per-unit perception cost above.

**The one guarantee you cannot make.** Do not conformalise free-form LLM generation and claim a coverage guarantee — autoregressive generation is conditional and **breaks exchangeability**, and a reviewer who knows the field will catch it ([TACL survey](https://aclanthology.org/2024.tacl-1.82.pdf)). The defensible design is to conformalise a classifier's scores over a **fixed, i.i.d.-sampled labelled set**. On sizing, the guidance is explicit and this is the highest-confidence finding in the research: the conformal threshold is the **⌈(n+1)(1−α)⌉/n** empirical quantile; marginal coverage is bounded as **1−α ≤ P(Y ∈ C(X)) ≤ 1−α+1/(n+1)**; achieved coverage is distributed **Beta(n+1−l, l)** with l = ⌊(n+1)α⌋; and the published table at δ=0.1, α=0.1 gives **n=22 for ±0.1 slack, n=102 for ±0.05, n=2,491 for ±0.01**, with n=1,000 recommended "for most purposes" and realised coverage then typically between 0.88 and 0.92 ([arXiv 2107.07511](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.07511)). Target **n ≈ 100–200 at α = 0.1** and claim ±0.05, not ±0.01. Report the **Beta interval for your actual n** rather than asserting "we achieve 90% coverage" — that single move converts a weak small-sample position into a rigorous one. One flag the researcher was careful about: **the floor n ≥ 1/α − 1 (so n ≥ 9 at α=0.1) is a derivation, not a quoted statement** from that paper — below it the quantile is undefined and the method silently returns the full label set. Report **language-stratified and class-stratified coverage (FSC), not only the marginal number**, because Zerva & Martins found uneven coverage across languages — marginal coverage can sit at 90% while Hinglish-heavy inputs are covered at 70%. Your calibration set must be exchangeable with deployment: **if you calibrate on Telegram scrapes and deploy on WhatsApp forwards, the guarantee does not transfer.** And note a genuine formalism distinction — **failing to flag a real scam is the costly asymmetric error, and bounding that rate is conformal risk control via Learn-then-Test, not vanilla conformal prediction** ([arXiv 2110.01052](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.01052)). Conflating the two in your writeup would be a real technical error. For the abstention mechanism itself, the **set-size rule — abstain when |C(x)| ≠ 1** — is right at your scale: it needs no calibration beyond the single conformal threshold, inherits the guarantee directly, and explains in one sentence to a panel. There is **no published norm for an acceptable abstention rate**, so whatever you target is a design decision to defend, not a benchmark to meet.

## Build the registry join first, the Hinglish test second, the demo third

Day 0 is three tests and one email, none of which require a model. **Open `OtherAction.do?doRecognisedFpi=yes&intmId=13` in a browser with dev tools and capture the POST body behind `searchFormFpi()` and `searchAllIntm()`** — if "Show All Records" dumps all 1,062 rows in one response, the registry is two requests instead of 136. **Download and parse `prs_ra_sebi.xls` and `prs_ra_others.xls`** to learn their column schema, which determines whether your enforcement join is key-based or purely fuzzy. **Read `sebi.gov.in/robots.txt`** before any crawler runs. And **send SEBI the reproduction-permission email** — it costs nothing and removes the main legal objection to caching their data.

Day 1 is Gate 2, and it gates everything downstream. **Provision a Meta Model API key and confirm from India that it works** (general availability is officially unconfirmed). Then run a **30-example Hinglish smoke test**: real voice notes through Muse Voice Transcribe scored with transliteration-aware matching rather than raw WER, and the same content as text through **Muse Spark 1.2** — not 1.3, whose audio understanding is documented as not fully supported. Decide your fallback before you need it. If ASR is unusable on Hinglish voice notes, the honest pivot is to make text and Shorts the primary path and present voice as a measured limitation — which is a better submission than a broken voice demo.

From there the ordering is forced by your own stated discipline: **rubric before labels, labels before calibration, calibration before any coverage claim.** Write the label taxonomy first, and make it act-type-and-status rather than truth: a single Hinglish tip typically bundles a factual claim ("this stock gave 300% last year"), a recommendation act ("buy now"), and a guarantee ("assured returns"), and those need three different verification paths — the first is checkable against market data, the second is a regulatory-status question answered by your registry join, and the third is a **per-se red flag under the Advertisement Code** requiring no truth judgement at all. That decomposition is justified by the regulatory structure, not by a general claim that decomposition helps fact-checking; note that **"Decomposition Dilemmas" frames that general benefit as contested, and its actual conclusion was not retrieved — do not assert which way it lands** ([arXiv 2411.02400](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.02400v2)).

The architecture that follows: Muse Voice Transcribe for voice notes and SAM 3.1 over sampled YouTube Short frames to detect — or fail to detect — the required on-screen disclosure overlay, with Muse Spark 1.2 doing multimodal claim extraction into a structured schema; a deterministic compliance checker running the Advertisement Code checklist (registered name, registration number, 10-point warning present and translated for non-English, illustration-only labelling, website hyperlink, plus the three-month price-lag test); a local registry index joined against the NSE debarment lists plus the cancelled/suspended page; and a conformal layer over a small classifier's scores — not over Spark's generations — with the set-size abstention rule. Consider a **small fine-tuned code-mixed encoder (HingBERT-family) as a first-stage filter**, since the published 15-point gap over zero-shot frontier prompting on code-mixed NER is the predictable criticism of a pure-LLM pipeline and this is the predictable answer. Keep Muse Wonder out unless you genuinely need long-horizon agentic coding; it is 4.7x Spark's output price for the same modalities and context. Keep Muse Image out entirely — you are in India.

Four open items to carry rather than quietly drop: the primary **SEBI Investor Survey 2025** report (it would convert your weakest evidentiary leg into a regulator-sourced statistic on finfluencer reliance); the **circular number for the 29 Jan 2025 finfluencer circular** and confirmation that the 2023 Advertisement Code is unamended; **arXiv 2512.04566 on conformal guarantees with small datasets**, which is likely the single most relevant paper for your calibration-size question and was not retrieved; and a **direct GitHub code search** for SEBI scrapers, since the "no repos exist" finding is web-search-only and moderate confidence.

## Conclusion

The most useful thing this research produces is not a green light — it is a correctly placed one. **KASAUTI's feasibility risk and its positioning risk sit in different places than they appear to.** The data access that looks hardest (a regulator with no API) is in fact a day of plumbing, while the thing that sounds like a feature (mapping a social handle to a legal entity) is unsolved by any source found and will consume most of ten days. Symmetrically, the capability that Meta's documentation appears to grant you — Hindi support, code-switching support — turns out to be a **support list with no number behind it**, so the highest-information action available on day 1 costs thirty examples and an afternoon.

The deeper point is about what kind of claim survives scrutiny in October 2026. Truth-checking financial claims is a benchmarked, leaderboarded field whose winners reach Micro-F1 0.8467 with GPT-4 in the loop; credential lookup belongs to SEBI as of 1 October 2025. What remains unclaimed is narrower and less glamorous: **treating "is this utterance a regulated recommendation from an unregistered speaker" as a classification problem, in code-mixed input, with a stated abstention rate** — a task the world's securities regulators are currently performing with keyword scrapers and manual filtering, and which IOSCO lists as an aspiration rather than a practice. That is a smaller claim than "AI that detects financial misinformation," and it is the only version of the pitch that a judge who checks your sources will not be able to take apart. The product that abstains, cites SEBI as of a date, and says "missing required disclosures" instead of "fraud" is both the legally safer product and the more impressive one — and the name *kasauti*, a touchstone that tests rather than convicts, already says so.
