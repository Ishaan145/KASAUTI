# Financial Misinformation and Unregistered Investment Advice Targeting Indian Retail Investors — Quantified Harm Evidence Pack (current to October 2026)

> **Note on currency:** Research conducted 26 October 2026. The most recent SEBI derivatives study available is dated August 2026 (covering FY25–FY26). Several figures below carry explicit "as of" dates; where a figure is an estimate, projection, or single-source claim it is flagged inline.

## Q1. How large is India's retail investor base now, and how fast has it grown since 2020?

### Takeaway
NSE's unique registered investor base (unique PANs) crossed 13 crore (130 million) on 27 April 2026, up from 1 crore reached only after ~14 years of operation; total NSE client accounts crossed 26 crore in mid-2026. The base compounded at 26.4% CAGR over FY21–FY26, roughly 1.7x the prior five-year rate, with growth now concentrated in Tier 2/3/4 cities and among under-30s.

### Cited Findings
- NSE unique registered investors (unique PANs) crossed **13 crore (130 million) on 27 April 2026** — [NSE Press Release, 27 Apr 2026](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/web/pressrelease/2026-04/PR_cc_27042026_20260427192805.pdf)
- The prior milestone of **12 crore was hit in September 2025** — the latest crore took about seven months — [NSE Press Release, 27 Apr 2026](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/web/pressrelease/2026-04/PR_cc_27042026_20260427192805.pdf)
- Pace of accretion: the **first crore took ~14 years** from start of operations; the next three crore took ~11 years in total; recent crores are being added roughly **every 6–8 months** — [NSE Press Release, 27 Apr 2026](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/web/pressrelease/2026-04/PR_cc_27042026_20260427192805.pdf)
- **Investor base CAGR of 26.4% over FY21–FY26**, versus 15.2% over FY16–FY21 — [NSE Press Release, 27 Apr 2026](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/web/pressrelease/2026-04/PR_cc_27042026_20260427192805.pdf)
- Total NSE **client codes (accounts) stood at 25.7 crore as of 25 April 2026**; NSE itself notes this is *not* a count of unique investors because one person can register with multiple trading members — [NSE Press Release, 27 Apr 2026](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/web/pressrelease/2026-04/PR_cc_27042026_20260427192805.pdf)
- NSE **accounts crossed 26 crore in June 2026**, adding the latest crore in under four months; unique registered investors were reported above 13.1 crore as of 31 May 2026 — [Daijiworld, Jun 2026](https://daijiworld.com/news/newsDisplay?newsID=1328169)
- Geographic spread as of **31 March 2026**: Maharashtra 2.0 crore investors, Uttar Pradesh 1.5 crore, Gujarat 1.1 crore; states outside the top 10 account for **27% of the investor base**; investors are present in **99.85% of India's pin codes** — [NSE Press Release, 27 Apr 2026](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/web/pressrelease/2026-04/PR_cc_27042026_20260427192805.pdf)
- Demographics: **median investor age ~33 years** (down from 36 in FY21); **nearly 40% of investors are under 30**; nearly **one in four is female** — [NSE Press Release, 27 Apr 2026](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/web/pressrelease/2026-04/PR_cc_27042026_20260427192805.pdf)
- **7.2 crore new SIP accounts** opened in FY26; average monthly SIP inflows rose from ₹3,660 crore (FY17) to **₹29,132 crore (FY26)** — [NSE Press Release, 27 Apr 2026](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/web/pressrelease/2026-04/PR_cc_27042026_20260427192805.pdf)
- **Mobile trading platforms account for more than a fifth of NSE cash market turnover** — [NSE Press Release, 27 Apr 2026](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/web/pressrelease/2026-04/PR_cc_27042026_20260427192805.pdf)
- Despite this growth, **only ~10% of Indian households invest in securities** (urban 15%, rural 6%) per the SEBI Investor Survey released 1 October 2025 — [Outlook Business, 1 Oct 2025](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/news/sebi-survey-finds-nearly-10-of-indian-households-invest-in-securities)
- A secondary source (blog, single-source — treat with caution) reports **~23 crore demat accounts** in India as of September 2026 — [Nooresh Tech blog, Sep 2026](https://www.nooreshtech.co.in/2026/09/india-has-23-crore-demat-accounts-but-how-many-investors-actually-show-up.html)

### Inferences
- The "26 crore accounts" and "13 crore unique investors" figures are frequently conflated in press coverage. For a judge-facing claim, **13 crore unique investors (NSE, April 2026)** is the defensible number; account counts roughly double-count.
- Because ~40% of investors are under 30 and the median age has fallen three years since FY21, the marginal new investor is young, mobile-first, and by definition has no experience of a full market cycle — the demographic most exposed to social-media investment content.
- Combining the NSE 13-crore base with the SEBI survey's ~10% household participation implies very large remaining headroom, so the exposed population is still expanding rather than saturating.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve official **CDSL/NSDL demat account totals** for a precise October 2026 figure directly from the depositories. The 23-crore figure is a single blog source.
- NSE's release gives CAGR but **no year-by-year unique-investor counts for 2020, 2021, 2022** — only milestone dates. A clean 2020-vs-2026 investor count comparison from a primary source was not located.

## Q2. What has SEBI quantified about retail investor losses, especially in F&O?

### Takeaway
SEBI's own studies are the single strongest quantified-harm evidence available: roughly 88–93% of individual equity-derivatives traders lose money every year, with aggregate net losses of ₹1.05 lakh crore in FY25 alone and over ₹1.8 lakh crore cumulatively across FY22–FY24. Losses are concentrated in the smallest, youngest, lowest-income traders.

### Cited Findings

**Latest study — SEBI, "Trading Behaviour of Individual Traders in the Equity Derivatives Segment (FY25–FY26)", August 2026 (PRIMARY):**
- **87.7% of individual EDS traders were loss-makers in FY26** (population of 78.6 lakh traders); the 5,050-trader analytical sample showed 88.1% — [SEBI study, Aug 2026](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787233601328.pdf)
- **91.1% were loss-makers in FY25** (sample basis) — [SEBI study, Aug 2026](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787233601328.pdf)
- **~93% of traders made losses after transaction costs during FY22–FY24** — [SEBI study, Aug 2026](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787233601328.pdf)
- **Aggregate net losses exceeded ₹1.8 lakh crore (₹1.8 trillion, ~USD 21bn) across FY22–FY24** — [SEBI study, Aug 2026](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787233601328.pdf)
- **1.23 crore unique individuals** traded EDS across FY25–FY26 — [SEBI study, Aug 2026](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787233601328.pdf)
- Top-15-broker trader population by year: FY22 51.35 lakh → FY23 66.96 lakh → FY24 95.75 lakh → FY25 106.30 lakh → **FY26 87.71 lakh** (first decline) — [SEBI study, Aug 2026](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787233601328.pdf)
- **Small traders are hit hardest:** 77% of traders deployed under ₹1 lakh of peak margin; they were 8% of turnover but **14% of losses**, with a **90% loss rate** versus 67.6% for the ₹10 lakh–₹1 crore bracket — [SEBI study, Aug 2026](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787233601328.pdf)
- **Young traders:** those under 30 had a turnover-to-portfolio ratio of ~93x and a loss-maker share of **~89%** — [SEBI study, Aug 2026](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787233601328.pdf)
- **Low-income traders:** those earning under ₹5 lakh/year ran turnover-to-portfolio ratios of ~75x, and **loss rates did not fall materially with higher income** — [SEBI study, Aug 2026](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787233601328.pdf)
- **Churn/replacement:** of FY25 first-time traders, only 44.1% were still trading after one year; of FY24 entrants, 37.4% after two years; **20.77 lakh first-time entrants in FY26** — [SEBI study, Aug 2026](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787233601328.pdf)
- **Losses recur:** ~90–92% of traders who lost in both prior years lost again — [SEBI study, Aug 2026](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787233601328.pdf)
- Options sellers who lost money averaged **₹51.7 lakh of loss per loss-maker** in FY26, versus ₹4.6 lakh for options buyers and ₹1.3 lakh for options-only buyers — [SEBI study, Aug 2026](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787233601328.pdf)

**Prior study — SEBI Press Release No. 39/2025, 7 July 2025 (covering FY25):**
- **Share of individual traders making net losses:** FY22 90.2% → FY23 91.7% → FY24 91.1% → **FY25 91.0%** — [TaxGuru summary of SEBI PR 39/2025, 7 Jul 2025](https://taxguru.in/sebi/sebi-releases-study-equity-derivatives-trends.html)
- **Aggregate net P&L after transaction costs (₹ crore):** FY22 −40,824 → FY23 −65,747 → FY24 −74,812 → **FY25 −1,05,603** (a 41% YoY widening) — [TaxGuru summary of SEBI PR 39/2025](https://taxguru.in/sebi/sebi-releases-study-equity-derivatives-trends.html); FY24/FY25 figures corroborated by [Business Standard, 7 Jul 2025](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/net-losses-of-traders-in-fo-widens-in-fy25-sebi-study-125070701221_1.html)
- **Average net loss per trader (₹):** FY22 −95,517 → FY23 −1,12,677 → FY24 −86,728 → **FY25 −1,10,069** (+27% YoY) — [TaxGuru summary of SEBI PR 39/2025](https://taxguru.in/sebi/sebi-releases-study-equity-derivatives-trends.html)
- **Trader counts (lakh):** FY22 42.7 → FY23 58.4 → FY24 86.3 → FY25 96.0 (top-13-broker client base; market-wide ~107 lakh) — [TaxGuru summary of SEBI PR 39/2025](https://taxguru.in/sebi/sebi-releases-study-equity-derivatives-trends.html)
- Within FY25, unique individual traders fell from **61.4 lakh in Q1 to 42.7 lakh in Q4** (~30% decline) following SEBI's October 2024 derivatives curbs — [Business Standard, 7 Jul 2025](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/net-losses-of-traders-in-fo-widens-in-fy25-sebi-study-125070701221_1.html)

**Earlier SEBI studies:**
- A January 2023 SEBI study found **89% of retail equity F&O traders suffered losses** — [Business Standard, 25 Jan 2023](https://www.business-standard.com/article/markets/sebi-study-suggests-89-retail-traders-in-equity-f-o-suffered-losses-123012501466_1.html)
- A July 2024 SEBI study found **over 70% of intraday equity traders incurred losses in FY23** — [Business Standard, 24 Jul 2024](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/over-70-intra-day-traders-incur-losses-during-fy23-reveals-sebi-study-124072401110_1.html)
- Active derivatives traders **fell 18% in FY26 to 8.75 million** — [Business Standard, 20 Aug 2026](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/active-derivatives-traders-fall-18-in-fy26-to-8-75-million-sebi-study-126082001378_1.html)

### Inferences
- The headline figure most useful for a non-India audience: **SEBI's own regulator-published data shows ~9 in 10 retail derivatives traders lose money, with ₹1.05 lakh crore (~USD 12bn) lost in a single financial year (FY25)**. This is a regulator's finding, not an advocacy estimate.
- The loss rate has been remarkably stable at 88–93% across five financial years and across multiple study designs — it is not an artefact of one year's market conditions.
- Harm is regressive: traders with under ₹1 lakh of capital are 77% of participants and have the highest loss rate (90%). This directly supports a consumer-protection (rather than sophisticated-investor) framing.
- The FY26 decline in trader counts (106.3 lakh → 87.7 lakh) coincides with SEBI's October 2024 derivatives tightening, which suggests regulatory intervention moves the needle on participation but has not meaningfully moved the **loss rate** (still 87.7%). This is an important nuance: supply-side curbs reduced exposure but not per-trader outcomes.

### Gaps
- The August 2026 SEBI study reports FY25/FY26 net losses on a **sample basis only** (₹12,718 lakh FY25, ₹12,407 lakh FY26 for the 5,050-trader sample), **not** a population aggregate. There is therefore **no published population-level aggregate net loss figure for FY26**. The latest population aggregate is FY25's ₹1,05,603 crore. The report-writer should not extrapolate the sample figures.
- SEBI does not publish a cumulative FY22–FY26 aggregate loss total.
- Internal inconsistency in the August 2026 SEBI document itself: the FY26 EDS trader base appears as 81.6 lakh in Section 5.21 but 78.6 lakh in Table 2 and 87.71 lakh in Table 1 (top-15-broker population). Flag this if a precise FY26 trader count is needed.
- **No SEBI study attributes derivatives losses causally to finfluencer content.** The F&O loss data establishes the magnitude of harm in the market that misinformation operates in; it does not by itself prove misinformation caused those losses. This is the single biggest evidentiary gap to be honest about.

## Q3. What enforcement has SEBI taken against finfluencers and unregistered advisers?

### Takeaway
SEBI has moved from case-by-case orders to platform-level takedowns: over 70,000 misleading handles/posts removed since October 2024, and headline cases escalating from ₹17.2 crore (Baap of Chart, Dec 2024) to ₹546 crore impounded (Avadhut Sathe, Dec 2025). In December 2025 SEBI gained direct IT Act takedown authority.

### Cited Findings

**Platform-level takedowns:**
- **Over 70,000 misleading handles and posts** taken down via cooperation with social media companies **since October 2024**, stated by **Ananth Narayan G, Whole-Time Member, SEBI**, on **21 March 2025** at an event for registered investment advisers in Mumbai — [Business Standard, 21 Mar 2025](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/unregistered-advisors-a-threat-70k-misleading-posts-removed-narayan-125032100779_1.html)
- Narayan described **unregistered advisers as a threat** to investors in the same remarks — [Business Standard, 21 Mar 2025](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/unregistered-advisors-a-threat-70k-misleading-posts-removed-narayan-125032100779_1.html)

**New takedown powers:**
- On **8 December 2025**, the Ministry of Finance notified SEBI as an **"authorised agency" under Section 79(3)(b) of the Information Technology Act, 2000**, read with Rule 3(1)(d) of the 2021 Intermediary Guidelines. This lets SEBI directly notify *any* intermediary to remove content violating securities laws; intermediaries must comply **within 36 hours** — [Mondaq analysis](https://www.mondaq.com/india/securities/1748526/how-sebis-takedown-powers-could-curb-online-investment-misinformation)
- The same analysis notes the notification **imposes no immediate compliance obligation** and that SEBI has published **no statistics on content removed under the new power** — [Mondaq analysis](https://www.mondaq.com/india/securities/1748526/how-sebis-takedown-powers-could-curb-online-investment-misinformation)

**Case 1 — "Baap of Chart" / Mohammad Nasiruddin Ansari (the flagship case):**
- SEBI's final order (reported **2–3 December 2024**) banned Ansari and **six others** from the securities market and ordered a refund of **₹17.2 crore (~USD 2.1 million)** collected as fees for unregistered investment advisory activity, within three months — [Moneylife, 3 Dec 2024](https://www.moneylife.in/article/sebi-bans-finfluencer-nasir-ansari-of-baap-of-charts-6-others-from-markets-asks-them-to-refund-rs172-crore-to-investors/75773.html); [Business Standard/PTI, 2 Dec 2024](https://www.business-standard.com/pti-stories/national/sebi-cracks-down-on-finfluencers-baap-of-charts-among-7-banned-for-up-to-1-year-124120201296_1.html); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/business/markets/indian-finfluencer-to-refund-21-million-to-followers-in-latest-crackdown-1.98992888)
- **Reach:** Ansari's YouTube channel had **443,000 subscribers and over 70 million views**; he also used Telegram, Instagram and X to distribute videos, subscription plans and course screenshots — [Moneylife, 3 Dec 2024](https://www.moneylife.in/article/sebi-bans-finfluencer-nasir-ansari-of-baap-of-charts-6-others-from-markets-asks-them-to-refund-rs172-crore-to-investors/75773.html)
- **Modus operandi per SEBI:** sold "educational" courses while giving specific buy recommendations in Bank Nifty options **via WhatsApp groups**; claimed **95% recommendation accuracy**, 20–30% personal profits, and promised **200–300% or assured returns** — [Moneylife, 3 Dec 2024](https://www.moneylife.in/article/sebi-bans-finfluencer-nasir-ansari-of-baap-of-charts-6-others-from-markets-asks-them-to-refund-rs172-crore-to-investors/75773.html)
- **The hypocrisy finding:** SEBI found Ansari had a **net personal trading loss of ₹2.89 crore between 1 January 2021 and 7 July 2023**, concealed from the investors he was advising; SEBI concluded the activity could not be "construed as being educational in nature" — [Moneylife, 3 Dec 2024](https://www.moneylife.in/article/sebi-bans-finfluencer-nasir-ansari-of-baap-of-charts-6-others-from-markets-asks-them-to-refund-rs172-crore-to-investors/75773.html)
- **Penalties:** Ansari ₹15 lakh + 1-year ban; Rahul Rao Padamati ₹5 lakh + 6-month ban; five other noticees (Tabraiz Abdullah, Asif Iqbal Wani, Golden Syndicate Ventures Pvt Ltd, Mansha Abdullah, Jadav Vamshi) ₹2 lakh each + 6-month bans. *Note: the Moneylife headline cites ₹32 lakh total but the itemised amounts sum to ₹30 lakh; the discrepancy is unexplained* — [Moneylife, 3 Dec 2024](https://www.moneylife.in/article/sebi-bans-finfluencer-nasir-ansari-of-baap-of-charts-6-others-from-markets-asks-them-to-refund-rs172-crore-to-investors/75773.html)
- An earlier SEBI interim/cease-and-desist direction was issued in **October 2023**, with disgorgement of ~₹17 crore sought — [Business Standard, 25 Oct 2023](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/sebi-bars-finfluencer-from-securities-market-asks-to-disgorge-rs-17-cr-123102501080_1.html)
- **Recovery escalation:** May 2025 demand notice (15-day deadline, not complied with) → **July 2025 Attachment Order** freezing demat accounts, mutual fund holdings, bank accounts and locker access → September 2025 SAT order directing Padamati to remit ~₹1.2 crore (not complied with) → **15 December 2025 prohibitory order**. Total payable to SEBI is **~₹18.13 crore** including penalties, interest and costs; noticees barred from dealing with movable/immovable property and required to disclose all property including title deeds within two weeks — [MediaNama, Dec 2025](https://www.medianama.com/2025/12/223-sebi-recovery-proceedings-rs-18-crore-baap-of-chart/); [CAalley](https://www.caalley.com/news-updates/indian-news/sebi-launches-rs-18-crore-recovery-proceedings-against-baap-of-charts-finfluencer-others)
- The original SEBI order document is at [sebi.gov.in, Dec 2024](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/dec-2024/1733151002336_1.pdf) (not fetched in this pass)

**Case 2 — Avadhut Sathe / Avadhut Sathe Trading Academy (the largest case to date):**
- SEBI order dated **4 December 2025** barred Avadhut Sathe, the Avadhut Sathe Trading Academy (ASTA) and director Gouri Sathe from the securities market and ordered **₹546 crore impounded** (an impounding, not a penalty) — [Mondaq analysis](https://www.mondaq.com/india/securities/1726258/sebis-crackdown-on-finfluencers-regulations-and-enforcement)
- SEBI had issued Sathe a **formal warning in 2024** before this order — [Mondaq analysis](https://www.mondaq.com/india/securities/1726258/sebis-crackdown-on-finfluencers-regulations-and-enforcement)
- Also covered by [Business Standard, 5 Dec 2025](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/avadhut-sathe-finfluencer-sebi-ban-disgorgement-unregistered-advisory-125120500178_1.html)

**Other 2026 enforcement:**
- SEBI reported **freezing accounts** of finfluencer perpetrators as part of a crackdown, reported **22 May 2026** — [WebIndia123, 22 May 2026](https://news.webindia123.com/news/Articles/Business/20260522/4453310.html)
- SEBI **cancelled the registration of 68 investment advisers** over non-payment of renewal fees, reported **27 November 2025** — [NewsOnAir, 27 Nov 2025](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/sebi-cancels-registration-of-68-investment-advisers-over-non-payment-of-renewal-fees/)
- SEBI issued **Press Release 32/2026** warning the public against transacting in unlisted securities on unauthorised platforms — [CorpLawUpdates](https://www.corplawupdates.in/updates/sebi-warning-unlisted-securities-unauthorised-platforms-2026)
- SEBI is reported to be imposing **stricter verification checks on social media investment advertisements** via Google and Meta — [International News and Views](https://www.internationalnewsandviews.com/sebi-social-media-investment-ads-verification-google-meta-405855-2/)
- SEBI has a long enforcement history here: a **June 2016 public notice (PR 112/2016)** already listed 10 unregistered entities directed to cease investment advice and withdraw advertisements, and warned against tips received via SMS, websites and social networking media — [SEBI PR 112/2016, 9 Jun 2016](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/docfiles/34058_t.html)
- A **Tamil finfluencer (Harish)** died in circumstances that Indian press framed as exposing the enforcement gap in regulating financial advisers — [The News Minute](https://thenewsminute.com/tamil-nadu/tamil-finfluencer-harishs-death-exposes-enforcement-gap-in-regulating-financial-advisers-2)

### Inferences
- The enforcement arc is clear and datable: individual orders (2016–2023) → the Baap of Chart order as a template case (Oct 2023 interim, Dec 2024 final) → platform-scale takedowns (70,000+ from Oct 2024) → direct IT Act takedown authority (Dec 2025) → very large impoundings (₹546 crore, Dec 2025). This is a regulator visibly escalating because prior measures were insufficient.
- The **70,000 takedowns in roughly five months** (Oct 2024 – Mar 2025) is the strongest single number for the *volume* of violating content in circulation — it implies a content supply far beyond what case-by-case adjudication can address. That is a direct argument for an automated/tooling approach.
- The "₹546 crore impounded from one academy" versus "₹15 lakh penalty on Ansari" gap shows that penalties alone are small relative to the sums moved, and that SEBI now prefers disgorgement/impounding.
- The Baap of Chart facts — a 443k-subscriber channel pushing Bank Nifty options calls through WhatsApp while the operator was personally down ₹2.89 crore — are an unusually clean, citable illustration for a non-India audience.

### Gaps
- **No single official SEBI figure for the total number of entities acted against for unregistered advisory/finfluencer activity** was found. SEBI's annual reports likely contain this; the annual report PDF was not retrievable in this pass (sebi.gov.in press-release index returned a client error).
- No published count of takedowns under the new December 2025 IT Act power.
- No updated takedown count after the March 2025 "70,000" figure — so that number is ~19 months old as of October 2026 and is almost certainly a significant understatement now. Flag as a floor, not a current total.
- Number of investors harmed in the Baap of Chart case, and the course fee amounts charged, were not found in available sources.

## Q4. What is the current state of SEBI's finfluencer regulations — what was actually enacted?

### Takeaway
Two distinct instruments are in force: the **Advertisement Code for IAs/RAs (Circular SEBI/HO/MIRSD/MIRSD-PoD-2/P/CIR/2023/51, issued 5 April 2023, effective 1 May 2023)**, which sets mandatory post/ad disclosures; and **regulations in force from 29 August 2024** barring regulated entities from associating with unregistered finfluencers, operationalised by a **29 January 2025 circular** restricting the use of live stock data by "educators."

### Cited Findings

**What a registered finfluencer/IA/RA must display (the specific answer to "what exactly must be on a post"):**
Per Circular **SEBI/HO/MIRSD/MIRSD-PoD-2/P/CIR/2023/51**, issued **5 April 2023**, effective **1 May 2023** — [SEBI Advertisement Code circular (PDF)](https://aria.org.in/beta/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/SEBI-Advertisement-code-for-Investment-Advisers-IA-and-Research-Analysts-RA1.pdf):
- Name **as registered with SEBI**
- Registered office address
- **SEBI registration number**
- Logo, brand name or trade name
- CIN, if applicable
- The **standard warning in at least 10-point font**, with no words added or removed: *"Investment in securities market are subject to market risks. Read all the related documents carefully before investing."*
- For **audio-visual** ads: the warning must appear on screen **and** be voiced over clearly and audibly
- For **non-English** ads: the warning must be accurately translated
- For **SMS, pop-up or social media** posts that cannot carry the full details: a **hyperlink to the official website** must be included, and that website must carry all the details, including the supervisory-body membership number (e.g. BASL for IAs)
- If specific securities appear as examples, the post must state they are **"for illustration only"** and not recommendatory
- A disclaimer that SEBI registration, BASL membership and NISM certification **do not guarantee performance or assured returns**
- **Prior approval** from a SEBI-recognised supervisory body (e.g. BASL) is required before issuing an advertisement
- Advertisement copies must be **retained for five years**

**Prohibited in such posts** — [same circular](https://aria.org.in/beta/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/SEBI-Advertisement-code-for-Investment-Advisers-IA-and-Research-Analysts-RA1.pdf): false, misleading, biased or deceptive statements including those based on assumptions or projections; misleading testimonials; exaggerated claims; language exploiting investor inexperience; excessive technical or legal jargon; calling a service "free" unless it truly is; promises or implications of **assured, minimum or target returns or risk-free advice**; disparaging other intermediaries or unfair comparisons; **reference to the IA's/RA's own past performance**; superlatives such as "Best" or "No. 1" (factual award details from independent organisations are allowed); and **use of the SEBI logo**. Suspended IAs/RAs may not advertise during suspension. No prize/medal/gift schemes.

**The association ban and the "educator" restrictions:**
- SEBI stated that the finfluencer-related regulations **came into force with effect from 29 August 2024** — [Business Standard, 30 Jan 2025](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/sebi-finfluencer-circular-live-stock-data-market-education-rules-125013000571_1.html)
- Norms restricting finance influencers' recommendations were **announced 27 June 2024** — [Business Standard, 27 Jun 2024](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/news/sebi-announces-norms-to-restrict-finance-influencer-s-recommendations-124062701002_1.html)
- A circular dated **29 January 2025** operationalised these: "educators" **may not use live stock prices** and may use only **price data with a three-month lag**; may not use stock names, codes or recent price data in a way suggesting future prices, advice or recommendations; and may not give investment advice or make performance claims without SEBI approval. Investor education itself remains permitted. **Registered market entities may not associate with finfluencers in any way involving monetary or non-monetary compensation.** Violators risk penalties, suspension or cancellation of their SEBI licence — [Business Standard, 30 Jan 2025](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/sebi-finfluencer-circular-live-stock-data-market-education-rules-125013000571_1.html)
- SEBI also issued an **April 2025 circular on frauds** instructing investors to verify registration before acting on investment tips — [Business Standard, 20 Apr 2025](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/personal-finance/sebi-circular-on-frauds-verify-registration-before-taking-investment-tips-125042000636_1.html)
- Secondary analyses of the 2026 state of the rules (what CAs, IFAs and registered advisers can and cannot post) — [Dealplexus](https://www.dealplexus.com/blog/sebi-finfluencer-rules); [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/sebi/death-get-rich-quick-stock-tips-sebi-s-regulations-finfluencers.html); [BW Marketing World](https://www.bwmarketingworld.com/article/sebi-tightens-grip-on-finfluencers-with-new-regulations-524518)

### Inferences
- The **three-month data lag rule** is the most consequential and most machine-checkable provision: a post showing live or recent prices alongside a stock name is prima facie non-compliant unless the poster is SEBI-registered. This gives a compliance tool an objective, automatable test rather than a subjective judgement of "advice."
- The disclosure requirements (registered name + SEBI registration number + 10-pt warning + illustration-only labelling + website hyperlink for social posts) are explicitly enumerated and therefore directly checkable from post text — including the requirement that the **warning be translated for non-English ads**, which is the hook connecting the regulation to the vernacular dimension.
- SEBI's approach is **indirect**: it regulates registered entities and platforms, not unregistered finfluencers themselves (who are outside its registration perimeter and reachable only through enforcement orders and takedowns). That structural gap is why takedowns scaled to 70,000+ and why the IT Act authorisation was needed.

### Gaps
- The exact **circular number and gazette date of the 29 January 2025 finfluencer circular** was not captured; Business Standard reported its content without the reference number.
- Could not verify from a primary SEBI source whether the Advertisement Code's disclosure list was amended between 2023 and October 2026. The 5 April 2023 circular is the version available; treat it as current but note it was not re-confirmed against sebi.gov.in in this pass (the SEBI press-release index page returned a client error).
- No data found on **compliance rates** with the disclosure requirements — i.e. what share of registered advisers' posts actually carry the required elements.

## Q5. Is there data on investment fraud/scam losses in India (NCRP, I4C, RBI, police)?

### Takeaway
I4C/NCRP data show cyber fraud losses of ₹52,976 crore cumulatively over 2020–2025, with **investment/trading scams accounting for 77% of all 2025 financial losses** — making investment fraud, by the government's own categorisation, the single largest category of cyber-financial harm in India.

### Cited Findings
- Data compiled by the **Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C)** under the Union Home Ministry, from complaints on the **National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal (NCRP)** — [The420.in](https://the420.in/india-cyber-fraud-52976-crore-i4c-data-investment-scams/)
- **Cumulative cyber fraud losses 2020–2025: ₹52,976 crore** — [The420.in](https://the420.in/india-cyber-fraud-52976-crore-i4c-data-investment-scams/)
- Losses and complaints by year — [The420.in](https://the420.in/india-cyber-fraud-52976-crore-i4c-data-investment-scams/):

| Year | Losses (₹ crore) | Complaints |
|---|---|---|
| 2020 | 8.56 | 1.27 lakh |
| 2021 | 551 | 2.62 lakh |
| 2022 | 2,290 | 6.94 lakh |
| 2023 | 7,463 | 13.10 lakh |
| 2024 | 22,849 | 19.18 lakh |
| 2025 | 19,812 | 21.77 lakh |

- **Investment/trading scams accounted for 77% of total 2025 financial losses** — the largest single category. Other 2025 shares: digital arrest 8%, credit card fraud 7%, sextortion 4%, e-commerce 3%, app/malware 1% — [The420.in](https://the420.in/india-cyber-fraud-52976-crore-i4c-data-investment-scams/)
- Applying 77% to ₹19,812 crore yields **~₹15,255 crore of investment-fraud losses in 2025** — **this is a derived calculation, not a published I4C figure**. Flag as an estimate.
- Top-five states accounted for ₹10,328 crore of 2025 losses — [The420.in](https://the420.in/india-cyber-fraud-52976-crore-i4c-data-investment-scams/)
- A separate report of the 2025 figure: Indians lost over **₹198 billion (₹19,800 crore) to financial fraud in 2025** per I4C and NCRP data — [FastBull/BrokersView](https://www.fastbull.com/brokersview/news/indians-lost-over-%E2%82%B9198-billion-to-financial-fraud-in-2025-i4c-ncrp-data-show-319040)
- **₹7,130 crore was saved/intercepted** from cyber fraud in 2025 — [Ministry of Cyber Affairs](https://ministryofcyberaffairs.com/news/india-s-cyber-fraud-fightback-7-130-crore-saved-in-2025-1dfbe88e-73ab-430f-8864-5c772e81a286)

- Separate CyberPeace analysis based on **Rajya Sabha proceedings and MHA data** on India's cybercrime surge — [CyberPeace](https://cyberpeace.org/resources/blogs/cyberpeace-analysis-indias-cybercrime-surge-signals-a-growing-digital-security-challenge-an-assessment-based-on-rajya-sabha-proceedings-and-mha-data)
- Background on rising cyber frauds — [Drishti IAS](https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-updates/daily-news-analysis/rising-cyber-frauds-in-india)
- A separate FCRF analysis attributes **36% of fraud with Southeast Asian links to investment fraud** — note this uses a **different base** and must not be combined with the 77% figure — [The420.in](https://the420.in/india-cyber-fraud-52976-crore-i4c-data-investment-scams/)

### Inferences
- The growth curve is the striking part: from ₹8.56 crore (2020) to ₹22,849 crore (2024) — a ~2,600x increase in five years, during exactly the period the retail investor base tripled. Complaints rose from 1.27 lakh to 21.77 lakh over the same window.
- 2025 losses (₹19,812 crore) were **lower** than 2024 (₹22,849 crore) even as complaints rose (19.18 → 21.77 lakh), implying a falling average loss per complaint. Possible explanations include interception (₹7,130 crore saved) and a shift toward higher-volume/lower-value fraud — but this is unverified speculation and should be flagged as such if used.
- Taken together with the SEBI F&O data, two independent government data streams point at the same population: SEBI's ₹1.05 lakh crore of legal-but-ruinous derivatives losses (FY25), and I4C's ~₹15,000 crore of outright investment fraud (CY2025). These are separate harms and should not be summed.

### Cautions on these sources
- All I4C/NCRP figures here come via **news aggregation of I4C data, not a primary I4C publication**. The420.in cites no specific I4C report or Parliament reply. Treat as reliable-but-secondary.
- The420.in's own article contains internal inconsistencies: it calls ₹19,812 crore "nearly one-third" of the six-year total (it is ~37%), calls the 2024 figure "marginally higher" (it is ~₹3,000 crore higher), and misattributes the top-five-states share. The underlying table appears sound; the narrative framing is loose.
- NCRP data are **reported-complaint** data and therefore understate true harm (under-reporting of investment fraud is widely acknowledged, though I found no quantified under-reporting estimate).

### Gaps
- **No primary I4C or MHA publication** retrieved. A Parliament answer or I4C annual report would be the better citation and should be sought if available.
- **No official I4C rupee figure specifically for "stock trading scams"** as a named category — only the 77% share of 2025 losses for the broader "investment scam" category.
- No RBI-published figure on investment/securities fraud losses was located (RBI fraud reporting covers bank frauds, a different category).
- No 2026 year-to-date I4C figures found (data runs to CY2025).

## Q6. What is known about the vernacular / regional-language dimension?

### Takeaway
Indian-language internet users overtook English users around 2016 and were projected to be ~75% of the base by 2021; local-language content is trusted *more* than English content by 68% of users. SEBI's own 2025 investor survey found a strong cross-segment preference for financial education in regional languages. However, I found **no study quantifying financial misinformation specifically in Hindi/regional languages or in voice notes** — this is the largest evidentiary gap in the pack.

### Cited Findings

**Scale of vernacular internet use (Google-KPMG, April 2017 — PRIMARY but dated):**
- **234 million Indian-language internet users at end-2016**, versus 175 million English users — Indian-language users had already overtaken English — [KPMG-Google, "Indian languages: Defining India's Internet", Apr 2017](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmg/in/pdf/2017/04/Indian-languages-Defining-Indias-Internet.pdf)
- **Projected to 536 million by 2021** versus 199 million English users — **~75% of India's internet user base**. *This is a 2017 projection, not an actuals figure — flag as a projection.* — [KPMG-Google, Apr 2017](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmg/in/pdf/2017/04/Indian-languages-Defining-Indias-Internet.pdf)
- Indian-language users grew at a **41% CAGR from 42 million (2011) to 234 million (2016)**; projected 18% CAGR 2016–2021 against ~3% for English — [KPMG-Google, Apr 2017](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmg/in/pdf/2017/04/Indian-languages-Defining-Indias-Internet.pdf)
- **68% of Indian internet users consider local-language digital content more reliable than English content** — this is the single most important finding for a misinformation-risk argument — [KPMG-Google, Apr 2017](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmg/in/pdf/2017/04/Indian-languages-Defining-Indias-Internet.pdf); corroborated in contemporaneous coverage — [Quartz India](https://qz.com/india/972844/indias-internet-users-have-more-faith-in-content-thats-not-in-english-study-says)
- **88–90% of Indian-language users are more likely to respond to a digital ad in their local language** than in English — [KPMG-Google, Apr 2017](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmg/in/pdf/2017/04/Indian-languages-Defining-Indias-Internet.pdf)
- **70% of Indian-language users struggle with English keyboards**; 60% cite limited language support/content as the largest barrier to adopting online services; 30% are aware of online content but not comfortable using the medium — [KPMG-Google, Apr 2017](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmg/in/pdf/2017/04/Indian-languages-Defining-Indias-Internet.pdf)
- **Finance-adjacent:** 47 million Indian-language users accessed digital payments in 2016, projected to 175 million by 2021 (~30% CAGR); their share of digital payments was projected to rise from 28% to 43%; ~58% still used only offline channels because of language barriers — [KPMG-Google, Apr 2017](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmg/in/pdf/2017/04/Indian-languages-Defining-Indias-Internet.pdf)
- A later KPMG M&E report projected **500 million users accessing the internet in local languages by 2030** — *projection* — [afaqs](https://www.afaqs.com/news/media/by-2030-500-million-users-will-access-internet-in-a-local-language-kpmg-me-report-2019); [Slator](https://slator.com/localization-is-shaping-the-future-of-digital-india-kpmg-report/); [Tech Observer](https://techobserver.in/news/enterprise-it/indian-language-users-rule-internet-india-google-kpmg-report-1745/)

**The regulatory hook:**
- SEBI's Advertisement Code requires that for **non-English advertisements the mandatory risk warning be accurately translated** — i.e. SEBI already contemplates vernacular financial promotion — [SEBI Circular SEBI/HO/MIRSD/MIRSD-PoD-2/P/CIR/2023/51, 5 Apr 2023](https://aria.org.in/beta/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/SEBI-Advertisement-code-for-Investment-Advisers-IA-and-Research-Analysts-RA1.pdf)
- For **audio-visual** content the warning must be both on screen and **voiced over clearly and audibly** — the closest thing to a regulatory touchpoint for audio/voice-note financial content — [same circular](https://aria.org.in/beta/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/SEBI-Advertisement-code-for-Investment-Advisers-IA-and-Research-Analysts-RA1.pdf)

**Demand-side evidence from the regulator:**
- The SEBI Investor Survey (released **1 October 2025**, conducted by **Kantar** with AMFI, NSE, BSE, NSDL and CDSL, sample **over 90,000 households across 400 cities and 1,000 villages**) found a **strong preference for financial education in regional languages across all segments**, and suggested localisation could be key to investor outreach and inclusion — [Outlook Business, 1 Oct 2025](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/news/sebi-survey-finds-nearly-10-of-indian-households-invest-in-securities)
- The same survey found **Gen-Z households favour short-form video**, while older groups prefer long-form content (articles, podcasts, workshops) — [Outlook Business, 1 Oct 2025](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/news/sebi-survey-finds-nearly-10-of-indian-households-invest-in-securities)
- The Baap of Chart case is itself a vernacular-adjacent data point: the operator branded in Hindi ("Baap of Chart" — literally "father of charts") and built a 443,000-subscriber YouTube following — [Moneylife, 3 Dec 2024](https://www.moneylife.in/article/sebi-bans-finfluencer-nasir-ansari-of-baap-of-charts-6-others-from-markets-asks-them-to-refund-rs172-crore-to-investors/75773.html)
- A Tamil-language finfluencer case (Harish) was reported as exposing the regulatory gap, indicating the phenomenon is not Hindi-only — [The News Minute](https://thenewsminute.com/tamil-nadu/tamil-finfluencer-harishs-death-exposes-enforcement-gap-in-regulating-financial-advisers-2)
- Secondary/marketing-industry sources on vernacular content dominance and voice search growth (low evidentiary weight; use only for colour) — [Social Beat](https://www.socialbeat.in/blog/kpmg-google-study-on-regional-content/); [Markhub24 on voice search](https://www.markhub24.com/post/voice-search-in-india-winning-the-next-wave-of-regional-queries); [Digital Zapp](https://digitalzapp.com/why-local-language-content-is-the-future-of-indian-digital-marketing/)

### Inferences
- The **68% "local language is more reliable" finding is the key causal link** in the vernacular argument: the audience most likely to encounter financial content in Hindi/regional languages is also the audience most predisposed to trust it. Combined with SEBI's finding that only 36% of investors have even moderate securities-market knowledge, this is a structurally high-risk combination.
- SEBI's own requirement to translate the risk warning and voice it over in audio-visual content implies the regulator recognises vernacular and audio channels as material — useful for arguing that a vernacular/voice-focused tool addresses a regulator-acknowledged surface, not an invented one.
- Because NSE's growth is now coming from Tier 2/3/4 cities and the Northeast (Mizoram 8.7x, Arunachal 7.9x, Assam 6.9x since FY21), the marginal new investor is increasingly likely to be a non-English-first internet user. This is a reasonable inference from two separate primary sources, but **no source directly measures the language profile of new investors**.

### Gaps
- **The Google-KPMG data is from April 2017 and its forward figures are 2021 projections.** No equivalent recent (2024–2026) primary measurement of the English vs Indian-language internet user split was located. This is a significant weakness: the headline vernacular statistics in this pack are ~9 years old. A judge-facing claim should date them explicitly rather than present them as current.
- **No study found that quantifies financial misinformation volume or prevalence by language.** I found no research measuring what share of Indian financial misinformation is in Hindi or regional languages.
- **No evidence found on voice notes as a vector for financial misinformation.** This was specifically asked for and I found nothing. If this is load-bearing for the tool's positioning, it should be presented as a reasoned hypothesis, not an evidenced claim.
- No measurement of regional-language financial content volume on YouTube/Instagram/WhatsApp.
- The SEBI Investor Survey's regional-language finding is reported qualitatively ("strong preference") in the source available — **no percentage** was captured. The primary survey report should be obtained for a citable number.

## Q7. WhatsApp and Telegram as vectors for investment fraud in India

### Takeaway
The modus operandi is well documented by police, the Enforcement Directorate and the MHA: victims are recruited via social media ads, moved into WhatsApp/Telegram groups impersonating real brokerages, shown fictitious gains in fake apps, then blocked at withdrawal. Individual losses run into crores; the ED found 200+ shell firms in one probe. What is missing is aggregate enforcement statistics.

### Cited Findings

**Documented modus operandi (ED-investigated, reported 9 September 2024):**
- Victims are contacted on **Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp and Telegram** with promises of high returns and IPO allotment "through special quota" — [Business Standard, 9 Sep 2024](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/personal-finance/fake-profits-to-whatsapp-stock-market-scam-decoded-124090900416_1.html)
- Victims are added to **WhatsApp/Telegram groups named to resemble real firms** — documented impersonations include **ICICI Securities, GFSL Securities and BlackRock Capital** — with planted members posting fake success stories — [Business Standard, 9 Sep 2024](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/personal-finance/fake-profits-to-whatsapp-stock-market-scam-decoded-124090900416_1.html)
- Scammers distribute **app links or APK files over WhatsApp**; the fake apps list stocks named after real companies (Reliance, Tata Power) — [Business Standard, 9 Sep 2024](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/personal-finance/fake-profits-to-whatsapp-stock-market-scam-decoded-124090900416_1.html)
- Early returns shown in the app are **entirely fictitious**; at withdrawal victims are asked for taxes or brokerage fees, then cut off — [Business Standard, 9 Sep 2024](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/personal-finance/fake-profits-to-whatsapp-stock-market-scam-decoded-124090900416_1.html)

**Documented individual losses (police complaints):**
- Noida resident "Trishala": **₹1 crore**, after being added to a WhatsApp group called "BlackRock Stock Club"; removed from the group when she tried to withdraw — [Business Standard, 9 Sep 2024](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/personal-finance/fake-profits-to-whatsapp-stock-market-scam-decoded-124090900416_1.html)
- Noida businessman: **₹9.09 crore** via a WhatsApp group named **"GFSL Securities official Stock C 80"** — the group name itself evidences the impersonation-plus-enumerated-group pattern — [Business Standard, 9 Sep 2024](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/personal-finance/fake-profits-to-whatsapp-stock-market-scam-decoded-124090900416_1.html)
- Faridabad resident: **₹7.59 crore** via apps "IC ORGAN MAX" and "Techstars.shop" after a Facebook link — [Business Standard, 9 Sep 2024](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/personal-finance/fake-profits-to-whatsapp-stock-market-scam-decoded-124090900416_1.html)
- Bathinda (Punjab) doctor: **₹5.93 crore** after downloading a fake "GFSL Securities" app — [Business Standard, 9 Sep 2024](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/personal-finance/fake-profits-to-whatsapp-stock-market-scam-decoded-124090900416_1.html)
- Noida resident Rohit Chawla: **₹40.70 lakh** — [Business Standard, 9 Sep 2024](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/personal-finance/fake-profits-to-whatsapp-stock-market-scam-decoded-124090900416_1.html)
- Mysuru man: **₹1.77 crore** in a fake trading app scam — [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/karnataka/mysuru/mysuru-man-loses-rs-177-crore-in-fake-trading-app-scam-3855312)
- Gujarat man: **₹1.46 crore** via a fake investment firm and WhatsApp groups; two arrested — [Punjab Kesari](https://english.punjabkesari.com/india/fake-investment-firm-whatsapp-groups-used-to-dupe-gujarat-man-of-rs-146-crore-two-held)
- Noida: Telegram and WhatsApp investment scams costing victims **over ₹90 lakh** via fake trading apps — [The420.in](https://the420.in/noida-investment-fraud-telegram-whatsapp-fake-trading-apps/)
- Goa senior citizen defrauded via a fake investment app; two arrested — [The420.in](https://the420.in/goa-online-investment-fraud-two-arrested/)
- Haryana: two arrested in a **₹6.2 lakh** Telegram fraud — [Tribune India](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/haryana/two-arrested-in-6-2-lakh-telegram-fraud)

**Enforcement and infrastructure:**
- The **Enforcement Directorate opened a PMLA money-laundering probe in early September 2024** based on multiple FIRs across India; found the accused used **200-plus shell firms**, some foreign-based; **four Bengaluru residents arrested** for allegedly siphoning ~**₹25 crore**. Investigators documented forged bank statements, rented mule accounts, transactions deliberately kept under ₹5 lakh to avoid alerts, and conversion of proceeds into cryptocurrency — [Business Standard, 9 Sep 2024](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/personal-finance/fake-profits-to-whatsapp-stock-market-scam-decoded-124090900416_1.html)
- The same reporting links the operations to **human trafficking** — Indians lured by job offers and trafficked to the Golden Triangle to staff scam compounds — [Business Standard, 9 Sep 2024](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/personal-finance/fake-profits-to-whatsapp-stock-market-scam-decoded-124090900416_1.html)
- The **Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA) issued a public warning** specifically about WhatsApp and Telegram investment scams promising "guaranteed" profits — [Asianet Newsable](https://newsable.asianetnews.com/india/whatsapp-telegram-investment-scams-mha-warns-of-guaranteed-profits-articleshow-zy4elyw); [The News Mill, Jul 2026](https://thenewsmill.com/2026/07/india-warns-against-investment-scams-on-whatsapp-and-telegram/)
- **Punjab Police busted a gang** offering high stock-market profits through Telegram and WhatsApp groups and issued a public advisory — [Royal Patiala](https://www.royalpatiala.in/gang-offering-high-profits-in-stock-market-investments-through-telegram-whatsapp-groups-busted-punjab-police-issues-advisory/)
- SEBI's **own enforcement** found Baap of Chart giving Bank Nifty options buy recommendations **via WhatsApp groups** — i.e. messaging apps are the delivery channel even for the "legitimate-looking" finfluencer cases, not just outright fraud — [Moneylife, 3 Dec 2024](https://www.moneylife.in/article/sebi-bans-finfluencer-nasir-ansari-of-baap-of-charts-6-others-from-markets-asks-them-to-refund-rs172-crore-to-investors/75773.html)
- SEBI's 2016 public notice already warned against tips received via SMS, websites and social networking media, though it did **not** name WhatsApp or Telegram — [SEBI PR 112/2016, 9 Jun 2016](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/docfiles/34058_t.html)

### Inferences
- The group-naming pattern ("GFSL Securities official Stock C 80", "BlackRock Stock Club") shows that **impersonation of real regulated brands is the core trust mechanism**, and that it is textual and therefore detectable — a group name and the presence/absence of a SEBI registration number are both machine-readable signals.
- The ED's finding of 200+ shell firms and four arrests recovering ~₹25 crore against individual victim losses of ₹9 crore and ₹7.59 crore indicates an industrialised operation where post-hoc enforcement recovers a small fraction. The implication for a prevention-side tool is direct.
- The documented losses span retail profiles from a ₹6.2 lakh victim to a ₹9.09 crore victim, so this is not confined to unsophisticated small investors.

### Gaps
- **No data found on typical WhatsApp/Telegram investment-fraud group sizes.** Asked for specifically; not available in any source retrieved. WhatsApp's technical group cap (1,024 members as of recent versions) is background knowledge, not a sourced finding about fraud groups, and should not be cited as such.
- **No aggregate enforcement statistics** — no count of fraud groups taken down, accounts banned, or FIRs filed specifically for messaging-app investment fraud. Only individual cases.
- No published figures from Meta/WhatsApp or Telegram on removal of investment-fraud groups in India.
- The richest single source (Business Standard, Sep 2024) is now ~2 years old. More recent cases exist but are individually reported; no updated synthesis was found.
- Pump-and-dump groups were asked about specifically; I found **no SEBI enforcement statistics on Telegram-based pump-and-dump schemes** in this pass, though such cases are known to exist.

## Q9. Comparable international regulatory action on finfluencers

### Takeaway
Finfluencer regulation is demonstrably a global, coordinated concern, not an Indian peculiarity: the FCA led a **June 2025 "week of action" with nine international regulators**, producing 3 arrests, 6 criminal proceedings and 650 social-media takedown requests.

### Cited Findings
- The **FCA's finfluencer "week of action" took place in June 2025 and involved nine international regulators**, producing: **3 arrests, 6 criminal proceedings, 11 targeted warning/cease-and-desist letters, 50 warning list alerts, and 650 social media takedown requests** — [FCA press release, 9 Jul 2026](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/fca-cracks-down-illegal-promotions-and-market-abuse-first-year-new-strategy) (published as part of the FCA's annual report and accounts)

### Gaps

- The identity of the nine regulators in the FCA's June 2025 week of action was not captured (notably: whether SEBI participated — which would be a valuable detail if true).

## Cross-cutting notes for the report-writer

### The strongest judge-facing claims, in order
1. **SEBI's own data: ~88% of individual equity-derivatives traders lost money in FY26; aggregate net losses were ₹1.05 lakh crore (~USD 12bn) in FY25 alone and over ₹1.8 lakh crore across FY22–FY24.** Regulator-published, multi-year, methodologically stable. [SEBI, Aug 2026](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787233601328.pdf)
2. **13 crore (130 million) unique Indian retail investors as of 27 April 2026, growing at 26.4% CAGR since FY21, median age 33.** [NSE, Apr 2026](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/web/pressrelease/2026-04/PR_cc_27042026_20260427192805.pdf)
3. **SEBI removed over 70,000 misleading social media handles/posts in roughly five months (Oct 2024 – Mar 2025).** The scale-of-content argument. [Business Standard, 21 Mar 2025](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/unregistered-advisors-a-threat-70k-misleading-posts-removed-narayan-125032100779_1.html)
4. **Investment/trading scams were 77% of all Indian cyber-financial fraud losses in 2025**, out of ₹19,812 crore total. [The420.in / I4C data](https://the420.in/india-cyber-fraud-52976-crore-i4c-data-investment-scams/)
5. **Only 36% of Indian investors have moderate securities-market knowledge, while ~80% of households prefer capital preservation** (SEBI Investor Survey, 90,000+ households, Oct 2025). [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/news/sebi-survey-finds-nearly-10-of-indian-households-invest-in-securities)
6. **Nine international regulators ran a coordinated finfluencer enforcement week in June 2025.** Establishes global recognition. [FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/fca-cracks-down-illegal-promotions-and-market-abuse-first-year-new-strategy)

### The three honest weaknesses
1. **No source causally links finfluencer content to the F&O losses.** The F&O data establishes the size of the pot; it does not prove misinformation caused it. Any claim that joins them is an inference and must be labelled as one.
2. **The vernacular statistics are from April 2017 and their forward figures are projections.** The 68% trust finding and the 234m/536m user figures are the backbone of the vernacular argument and they are ~9 years old. No recent primary replacement was found.
3. **No evidence at all on voice notes as a financial-misinformation vector, and none on messaging-group sizes.** Both were asked for. If the tool's positioning rests on either, it rests on a hypothesis.

### Figures that must not be combined
- SEBI F&O losses (₹1.05 lakh crore, FY25) and I4C investment-fraud losses (~₹15,000 crore, CY2025) are **different harms** — legal-but-ruinous trading versus criminal fraud. Do not sum.
- The I4C 77% share and the FCRF 36% Southeast-Asia-linked share use **different bases**. Do not combine.
- SEBI's 70,000 takedowns and the FCA's 650 takedown requests are **not methodologically comparable**.
- The August 2026 SEBI study's FY25/FY26 net loss figures (₹12,718 lakh / ₹12,407 lakh) are **sample-level, not population-level**. Do not present as national aggregates.
